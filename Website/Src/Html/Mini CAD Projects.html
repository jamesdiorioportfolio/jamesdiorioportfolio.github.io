Bracket for Workbench

Project name: Bracket for Workbench
Date: April 11, 2026
Category: Other - mechanical design

1. PROBLEM AND OBJECTIVE

Problem: The power-strip cable was thick and stiff, stuck out from the workbench, and made the workspace look untidy.

Objective: Hold the cable neatly against the side of the workbench while keeping the power strip usable.

2. TOOLS AND TECHNOLOGIES

Hardware, components and materials: Workbench, power strip and cable, 3D-printed bracket prototypes, and nuts and bolts. The printer model and filament are not listed.

Software and programming languages: CAD software was used, but its name is not listed. No programming was involved.

3. SOLUTION DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT

The bracket uses two halves and a groove to hold the cable against the bench. The design went through versions 0.1 and 0.2. Version 0.2 was made thicker, with revised snap-fit geometry, added reinforcement and higher infill. The write-up also notes changing to nuts and bolts.

4. CHALLENGES AND PROBLEM-SOLVING

The first prototype did not print correctly, and its snap fits broke. The next version revised the part thickness, snap-fit radius and reinforcement. The report says the revision resolved the problems.

5. TESTING AND RESULTS

The first prototype failed. The revised bracket held the cable against the workbench and improved cable management. The report includes no measurements or formal test procedure.

6. LESSONS LEARNED AND FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

The project built CAD and prototyping experience, especially around snap fits, reinforcement and iteration. Future work could document the exact fastener choice and compare the two versions with measurements.

7. INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTIONS AND RESOURCES

The write-up describes the design and revisions in first person and does not name collaborators. The workbench, power strip and cable defined the design requirements.

iPhone Holder for Wireless Charging

Project name: iPhone Holder for Wireless Charging
Date: August 25, 2026
Category: Other - mechanical design

1. PROBLEM AND OBJECTIVE

Problem: Phones and charging cables cluttered the kitchen bench. Moving them into a cabinet created a new question: how to mount the phones beside an outlet.

Objective: Design holders that keep phones in place while they charge and reduce clutter.

2. TOOLS AND TECHNOLOGIES

Hardware, components and materials: Phones, charging cables, an external outlet and the cabinet installation. The holder material and fabrication method are not specified in the write-up.

Software and programming languages: Onshape CAD; no programming was involved.

3. SOLUTION DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT

The holder was modeled in Onshape and developed over four iterations with different dimensions and approaches. The final design supports both magnetic and cable charging.

4. CHALLENGES AND PROBLEM-SOLVING

Finding a way to mount the phones and fit the charging connections required four design iterations. After the fourth version, the openings around the Lightning/USB connections were found to be oversized. The write-up says function was prioritized over appearance.

5. TESTING AND RESULTS

A phone can slide into the holder and charge. The installed design is described as robust and functional enough for daily use, though not perfect.

6. LESSONS LEARNED AND FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

The project built CAD experience and showed how measurements from phones and charging hardware can guide a fitted design. A future version could refine the oversized connection openings and appearance.

7. INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTIONS AND RESOURCES

The write-up describes designing the holder in first person and does not name outside assistance. The cabinet, outlet, phones and charging hardware set the design constraints.
